Implementing Data Nutrition Labels in DSpace

John Zhang, University of Oklahoma Libraries

Repositories are taking in more datasets, and depositors increasingly expect those datasets to be reused, including for training and evaluating machine learning models. Yet a typical dataset record in an institutional repository tells a prospective user very little about what is inside the files: how the data was collected, what populations or time periods it covers, what is missing, and what it should not be used for. That information usually lives in a README, if it exists at all.

Data Nutrition Labels, developed by the Data Nutrition Project, offer a standardized, at-a-glance summary of a dataset's provenance, composition, intended uses, and known limitations. This presentation describes how we brought that idea into SHAREOK, our DSpace 9.2 repository.

We will start with the rationale: why a structured label serves curators and end users better than free-text documentation, and how it supports responsible reuse without adding much burden at deposit. We will then walk through the implementation, covering the metadata fields we defined, how depositors and curators supply label content during submission and review, and how the label is rendered on the item page in the Angular frontend alongside the standard DSpace metadata. We will be candid about what took more effort than expected, including deciding which label sections to require and handling legacy datasets deposited before the label existed.

Next, we will look at early benefits: more consistent dataset descriptions, clearer conversations with depositors about limitations and sensitive content, and a record that is easier for both people and machines to evaluate.

We will close with possible future directions, such as exposing label fields through OAI-PMH and the REST API, using labels to support AI-readiness assessments, and offering the work as a reusable configuration for other DSpace sites.

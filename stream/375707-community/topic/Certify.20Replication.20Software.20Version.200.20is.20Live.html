Topic: Certify Replication Software Version 0 is Live

Katalina Toth (Nov 04 2026 at 16:14):

Hi all! Gary King, Yiqing Xu and Leo Yang have recently written a paper that proposes certifying that a given replication dataset produces the results it claims to, prior to publication at an archive (https://gking.harvard.edu/certify), and we have built it for Dataverse. It is live on demo.dataverse.org for anyone who might want to try it out.

What it does: the author uses their own AI assistant on their own computer to run the program, which reruns their analyses to ensure that their results are reproducible, and then again under a random seed the author could not have anticipated, packs the replication files into a single plain-text packfile, and submits a certificate. The archive seals it with a public timestamp, and, in Dataverse’s case, shows it as a protected "Replication Certificate" metadata section on the dataset, and anyone can check it later with our open-source checking program.

To try it: make a draft dataset on demo, click Edit Dataset, then Certify under Configure Options, and paste the page's form into an AI assistant that can run commands (Claude Code, for example). It asks for your folder and should be able to do the rest on its own; the seal takes one to three hours, then you publish as usual. Instructions, a sample dataset and a feedback form: https://certify.iq.harvard.edu

  1. On your draft's page, click Edit Dataset, then Certify under Configure Options.

  2. The Certify page opens. Click Copy the form and paste it into your AI assistant.

  3. Once the certificate is sealed and the dataset published, it shows in the Replication Certificate section under the dataset's Metadata tab.

To see a certified dataset on demo first, with its Replication Certificate section under the Metadata tab: https://demo.dataverse.org/dataset.xhtml?persistentId=doi:10.70122/FK2/MCQJ2I
On the Dataverse side nothing changes substantially: we added a metadata block, an external tool, two workflows and two settings.

It is a beta, so please tell me what you think and if you encounter any issues, at ktoth@iq.harvard.edu.
